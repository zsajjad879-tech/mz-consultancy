How to follow up on an overdue invoice

Before writing another reminder, confirm that the invoice is genuinely unpaid. A partial payment, credit note or unresolved question can change the correct next step.

1. Check the current balance

Compare the invoice with the latest accounting record. Subtract recorded payments and credits. Confirm the due date, recipient and any recent messages. Avoid sending a second reminder while another colleague is already discussing payment.

2. Separate disputes from late payments

A customer questioning the work or amount needs a named owner and a resolution process. Pause routine reminders while the dispute is being reviewed. A promise to pay should have a specific date and a scheduled review.

3. Ask a clear, courteous question

Identify the invoice, verified outstanding balance and due date. Ask when payment is expected. Invite the customer to share a payment reference if they have already paid or to explain any invoice question. Do not invent penalties or threaten legal action.

4. Record the response

Keep the next action, owner and promised date together. A statement that payment was made is a reason to reconcile the records, not sufficient evidence to mark an invoice paid.

5. Measure the work accurately

Separate verified cash receipts from credits, corrections and write-offs. Review time spent and unresolved exceptions. A lower outstanding balance does not automatically mean your reminders caused new revenue.
